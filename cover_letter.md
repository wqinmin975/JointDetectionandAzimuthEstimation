Dear Editors,

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as a research article in *Sensors*. The work addresses passive, low-cost counter-UAV sensing with microphone arrays built from MEMS sensors—an approach that requires no emission and operates at night and in poor visibility.

We confirm that neither the manuscript nor any parts of its content are currently under consideration for publication with or published in another journal. All authors have read and approved the manuscript, agree with its submission to *Sensors*, and assert that there is no dispute among the authors regarding its content or competing interests in its preparation. The manuscript reports only publicly available datasets and contains no confidential or undisclosed information.

**Scope and data.** This is fundamentally an *algorithm* paper. All experiments are conducted on (i) a controlled numerical simulation of a six-element MEMS ULA, and (ii) two publicly available, peer-referenced corpora: the Fraunhofer IDMT real-world drone-audio dataset (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.17525811) and the Western Sydney University UAV Acoustic Localization Dataset (DOI 10.26183/07nm-4p35). We deliberately scope the study to algorithm design and validation on these open datasets. A field test with our *own* in-house MEMS hardware prototype is planned but explicitly left to future work and is not claimed here; we state this limitation openly rather than presenting hardware results we do not yet have.

**Reproducibility.** Every table and figure in the manuscript is regenerated by the accompanying code: the synthetic study, the network curves of Figure 2 and Table 2 come from a single run of `python drone_acoustic_joint.py --mode sim`, which exports `data_snr.csv` and `table1_repro.csv`; the classical curves overlaid on Figure 2 are produced by `baseline_snr.py`, which exports `baseline_snr.csv`; and the WSU evaluation is produced by `wsu_eval.py` and `plot_wsu.py`. Every script fixes its random seed, so repeated runs reproduce the reported values exactly. The code package, together with the LaTeX source of the manuscript and the MDPI class files, is submitted as Supplementary Material.

**Principal innovations.**
1. An end-to-end multi-task network that *jointly* detects a drone and regresses its azimuth from the raw multichannel waveform of a six-element MEMS ULA, replacing the conventional decoupled detection–localization pipeline and preventing detection errors from propagating into localization.
2. An uncertainty-based dynamic loss-weighting scheme with a closed-form effective-weight ($\lambda_t$) analysis that makes the two task gradients' trade-off directly interpretable, avoiding manual loss-weight search.
3. A Neyman–Pearson operating-point selection that yields an SNR-robust detection threshold $\gamma^*$ for a prescribed false-alarm target, with the constancy of $\gamma^*$ characterised honestly.
4. A geometry-aware GCC-PHAT frontend plus the true cubic-array geometry that transfers the same backbone to recorded audio (IDMT zero-shot/fine-tuned, and WSU classical baselines with a real-world detection ROC that the IDMT set cannot provide).
5. A transparent ablation showing that *joint training*, not the weighting scheme, drives the accuracy gain, together with an explicit account of the simulation-to-reality acoustic gap, the in-sample upper bound of fine-tuning, and the absence of real false-alarm validation.
