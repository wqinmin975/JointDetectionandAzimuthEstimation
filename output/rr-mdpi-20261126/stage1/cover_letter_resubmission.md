# Cover Letter — Resubmission

**To:** The Editorial Office, *Sensors* (MDPI)  
**Manuscript ID:** sensors-4688906  
**Manuscript type:** Article — major revision, resubmission  
**Title:** Joint Detection and Azimuth Estimation of UAVs Using MEMS Microphone Arrays and Multi-Task Deep Learning: From Simulation to Real-World Recordings  
**Authors:** Qinmin Wang, Zixin Wang, Zhenzhen Hu, Hongyuan Wang, Liwei Tian\*

---

Dear Editors,

We are resubmitting the substantially revised version of manuscript sensors-4688906, *Joint Detection and Azimuth Estimation of UAVs Using MEMS Microphone Arrays and Multi-Task Deep Learning: From Simulation to Real-World Recordings*, following the major-revision decision. We thank the editors and the three reviewers for a careful and constructive assessment. Rather than only editing the text, we re-examined the underlying code and experiments; this process uncovered genuine implementation defects, which we have corrected, and all reported results have been regenerated so that every number in the revised manuscript is produced by the released code.

**Overall nature of the revision.** The changes fall into three groups: (i) corrections of experimental and code defects; (ii) new controlled experiments and statistical verification requested by the reviewers; and (iii) reframing of the manuscript so that its claims match the evidence, including explicit statement of negative findings. The principal changes are summarized below; a point-by-point response is provided separately in the accompanying Response to Reviewers.

**Experimental and code corrections.** We found and fixed five genuine defects: the two log-variance parameters of the uncertainty-based dynamic weighting were created *after* the optimizer and therefore never updated (they are now optimized, with final values −2.76 and +0.94); drone-absent samples carrying an artificial 0° target entered the regression loss (the loss is now masked to drone-present samples); training used a linear $\ell_1$ loss in degrees while evaluation used a circular metric (training now uses a masked 180°-periodic circular $\ell_1$); simulated inter-microphone delays were rounded to integer samples (they are now fractional, via a frequency-domain phase ramp consistent with the continuous-delay reference model); and the earlier "detection-only"/"localization-only" rows used an *untrained* output head. All experiments were rerun with the corrected code. We also clarified that the six-element raw-waveform network (36,078 parameters) and the 28-channel GCC-PHAT network (24,618 parameters) are two separately trained networks with no shared weights, rather than a weight-reused adapter.

**New controlled experiments and statistical verification.** We added properly trained single-task references, Solo-Det and Solo-Loc, and a paired benchmark on identical Monte Carlo realizations. With these correct controls, single-task localization reaches 5.95° and fixed equal weighting 5.62°, both at least as accurate as the joint dynamic model's 7.50° at −5 dB; the repaired dynamic rule therefore brings no accuracy advantage over fixed weighting. We further added a paired geometry-encoding ablation (600 trials at −5 dB; MAE 2.25° ± 1.79° to 2.95° ± 2.30°, a paired increase of +0.70°), full-circle retraining with unfurled 360° labels, and MEMS-impairment simulations covering channel failure, geometry calibration error and self-noise. An exploratory cross-domain detection feasibility test pairs 4890 IDMT drone-positive windows with 861 WSU background-only windows (5751 scored windows; AUC 0.998, Hanley–McNeil 95% CI [0.9974, 0.9991]); we state explicitly that the negatives are ambient recordings from a different array, so the near-perfect separation may partly reflect the domain gap. Comparisons are supported by paired t-tests, Wilcoxon and permutation tests, Holm–Bonferroni correction, Wilson and AUC confidence intervals, and frame-block bootstrap.

**Honest reframing of the manuscript.** We revised the abstract, introduction, related work, experimental setup, results, real-data section, figures and tables, wording, and limitations. A new label-trade-off table reports all figures as in-sample and on two non-interchangeable scales: unfurled 360° labels reduce full-circle MAE from 138.424° to 31.889°, while folded MAE rises from 19.093° to 24.416° in the same re-run. The 18.05° IDMT figure is presented as an optimistic, in-sample error on the 180° folded azimuth axis — not a 360° bearing result and not a generalization estimate. Figure 3 carries a prominent axis-truncation warning; Table 2 labels the untrained-head rows as diagnostic ablations only; and the limitations are reordered by engineering importance. The negative findings are stated in the abstract and the Section 6.1 summary: neither joint training nor dynamic weighting improves azimuth precision, the classical TDOA baselines sit near chance under the narrowband signal model, and MUSIC is more accurate than the learned estimator in that idealized setting. The WSU experiments are described as classical-baseline results only; the learned detector and regressor were not evaluated on that corpus, and the real false-alarm performance of the learned detector remains unvalidated.
